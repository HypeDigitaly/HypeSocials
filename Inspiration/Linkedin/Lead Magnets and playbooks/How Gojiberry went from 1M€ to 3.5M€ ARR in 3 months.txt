## Step 1 — Building the Content Strategy

Everything started with research.

We wanted to understand what was already proven to generate engagement with Gojiberry's exact buyers, instead of brainstorming ideas from scratch.

### 1. Analyze Gojiberry's historical content

First, we reviewed everything Gojiberry had already published.

We identified:

*   which posts generated the most engagement
*   which topics resonated
*   which formats worked
*   what their audience naturally interacted with

### 2. Benchmark the niche

Then we moved outside Gojiberry.

Using Favikon, we collected the highest-performing LinkedIn posts in their niche.

Specifically, we looked for posts around:

*   LinkedIn outreach
*   outbound prospecting
*   sales automation
*   lead generation

The goal wasn't inspiration.

It was to build a database of posts that had already generated hundreds or thousands of comments from the exact audience Gojiberry wanted to reach.

### 3. Extract the patterns with Claude

Once we had:

*   Gojiberry's best historical posts
*   the best-performing posts from the niche

we sent everything to Claude.

Instead of asking it to write posts, we asked it to identify:

*   recurring hooks
*   recurring structures
*   recurring content angles
*   recurring lead magnet formats

In other words:

_What patterns consistently produce engagement in this market?_

### 4. Build the campaign assets

From those patterns, Claude generated:

*   content pillars
*   post intentions
*   hook ideas
*   lead magnet ideas
*   supporting assets

At the end of this phase we already knew:

*   what topics we'd cover
*   which lead magnets we'd promote
*   which hooks we'd test
*   which assets creators would distribute

Everything else in the campaign was built from this foundation.

## Step 2 — Creator Activation

Once the content strategy was ready, we selected who would distribute it.

Today we work with a network of more than 5,000 LinkedIn creators.

Follower count wasn't our selection criteria.

Instead we looked at two things.

### ICP proximity

We wanted creators who looked like Gojiberry's buyers.

Typically:

*   founders
*   sales leaders
*   solopreneurs
*   SDRs
*   AE's

The closer a creator's audience was to Gojiberry's ICP, the better.

### Average engagement

The second criterion was simple:

_How much engagement does this creator consistently generate?_

A creator with 3,000 followers generating hundreds of comments every post is significantly more valuable than someone with 50,000 followers and little interaction.

Engagement quality mattered far more than audience size.

## Step 3 — Organic Distribution

Once creators were selected, we started distributing the content.

Almost every post was built around a lead magnet.

We mainly used two formats.

### Type A — Educational Lead Magnet

The post teaches part of the system.

Readers are then directed toward a detailed resource explaining step-by-step how to generate outbound leads using Gojiberry.

Usually this was hosted on a Notion page.

### Type B — Tool Reveal

The post showcases a strong result.

Instead of immediately revealing the tool, readers are asked to comment.

In exchange, they receive a tracked link leading directly to Gojiberry's signup page.

### Comment workflow

When someone commented, we had two possible flows.

Either:

*   reply publicly with a tracked link

or

*   send the resource by DM.

Depending on the campaign.

Those links pointed either to:

*   a detailed Notion guide
*   or Gojiberry's signup landing page.

Every click was tracked.

## Step 4 — Thought Leader Ads

Organic content gave us proof.

Paid distribution gave us scale.

Every winning post was turned into a Thought Leader Ad.

### Audience strategy

We built three types of audiences.

### ABM lists

Specific target companies.

### Buyer Groups

Lists of companies sharing similar characteristics.

### Classic LinkedIn targeting

Firmographics, job titles and company filters.

### Repetition over reach

The objective wasn't maximum reach.

It was maximum familiarity.

We deliberately kept audiences small enough that, given the available budget, our ICP would see Gojiberry content roughly 10 times per month.

Repeated exposure consistently outperformed showing one post once to a much larger audience.

## Step 6 — Attribution

Everything was measurable.

Pipeline influence · share% of attributed pipeline

### Tolt — affiliate & creator tracking

Gojiberry already had Tolt in place, so we plugged into it. For every post and every creator, we could track:

*   clicks
*   sign-ups
*   paying customers
*   revenue generated

This told us exactly which creators and which posts were driving actual revenue.

### What we usually set up on top

When a client doesn't already have Tolt, we typically deploy LeadJourney to do the same job — attributing clicks, sign-ups and paying customers back to each post and creator.

And on top of that, we generally add Fibbler to measure pipeline influence from our Thought Leader Ads. By connecting it to the CRM, we can see, for every deal and every customer, whether they've been exposed to our posts or clicked on our ads.

The point: we move beyond first-click / last-click and get a real view of LinkedIn's impact on pipeline and revenue.
